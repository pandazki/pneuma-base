# Provenance and lineage models, and the systems that implement them

Scope: vocabularies and formal models Pneuma Base could borrow for "item", "scene", "read", "made", "possibly stale" and "provenance unknown", and how established systems handle the hard cases. Researched 2026-11-25. Where a claim comes from a search-engine summary and not from a page I fetched, it is marked "(search snippet)". Pneuma terms used below: item (条目), scene (场景), scene card (场景卡), thread (线索), brought in / made (带进来的 / 做出来的).

## 1. W3C PROV (PROV-DM, PROV-O, PROV-CONSTRAINTS)

### Takeaway
PROV gives a ready vocabulary that matches Pneuma almost one to one: Entity (item), Activity (scene), Agent (agent or person), `used` (read), `wasGeneratedBy` (made). Two points matter most. First, PROV never lets one entity change: each state is a separate entity, linked to the enduring thing by `specializationOf`. Second, PROV says plainly that "used A and B, then generated C" does **not** by itself mean "C was derived from A and B". Derivation is a separate and stronger claim.

### Cited Findings
- PROV-DM and PROV-CONSTRAINTS are W3C Recommendations dated 30 April 2013. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)
- **Entity**: "a physical, digital, conceptual, or other kind of thing with some fixed aspects". **Activity**: "something that occurs over a period of time and acts upon or with entities", with optional start and end times. **Agent**: "bears some form of responsibility for an activity taking place". Agent subtypes are SoftwareAgent, Organization and Person. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Generation** (`wasGeneratedBy`) is the instantaneous completion of producing a new entity by an activity. **Usage** (`used`) is the instantaneous beginning of an activity's use of an entity, and one entity may be used many times. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Derivation** (`wasDerivedFrom`) is "a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity". The activity, the generation and the usage are all optional arguments. In the simplest form only the two entities are related. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- PROV-DM says a usage-then-generation chain through one activity is necessary but not sufficient for derivation. Derivation is "determined by unspecified means". — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- Subtypes of derivation: **Revision** (`prov:Revision`, "a revised version of some original"), **Quotation** (an entity repeats some or all of another by copying), and **PrimarySource**. PrimarySource is a relation and not an entity type, because an entity can be the primary source for one thing but not for another. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Attribution** (`wasAttributedTo`) ascribes an entity to an agent and implies an unspecified activity. PROV-DM presents it as the relation to use when the activity is unknown or irrelevant. **Association** (`wasAssociatedWith`) assigns an agent responsibility for an activity, optionally with a **plan** ("an entity that represents a set of actions or steps"). **Delegation** (`actedOnBehalfOf`) records that one agent acted for another, and the responsible agent keeps some responsibility. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Invalidation** (`wasInvalidatedBy`) is "the start of the destruction, cessation, or expiry of an existing entity by an activity". **Communication** (`wasInformedBy`) records that one activity used some unnamed entity generated by another activity. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Change over time.** Change is modelled with distinct entities, not with one entity whose state changes. PROV-DM's examples: a car in Boston and the same car in Cambridge are different entities, and so are a green and a red traffic light. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Specialization** (`specializationOf(infra, supra)`): the specific entity "shares all aspects of the latter, and additionally presents more specific aspects of the same thing". "The lifetime of the entity being specialized contains that of any specialization." Example: the BBC News home page for 2012-03-23 is a specialization of the BBC News page `bbc:news/`. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Alternate** (`alternateOf`): "Two alternate entities present aspects of the same thing." They may or may not overlap in time. Example: two working drafts of the PROV-DM report are alternates of each other, and both are specializations of the same web page. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Bundle**: "a named set of provenance descriptions, and is itself an entity", so provenance can itself have provenance. Example: one bundle is attributed to a person and another bundle to W3C. **Collection** / `hadMember(c, e)`: a collection may have members that are not listed. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Unknown arguments in PROV-N.** Arguments are positional. The placeholder `-` marks an argument that is absent, for example an unknown time or plan. For several relations at least one optional argument MUST be present. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **PROV-CONSTRAINTS, in brief.** It defines when a PROV instance is *valid* (a consistent history that is safe to reason over) and when two instances are *equivalent*. Both are defined through normalization: definitions expand shorthand, inferences add implied statements, and uniqueness constraints merge statements. Ordering, typing and impossibility constraints are then checked on the normal form. Two instances are equivalent when their normal forms are isomorphic up to renaming of existential variables. PROV instances "need not be valid". — [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)
- **Existential variables.** "An existential variable denotes a fixed object that exists, but its exact identity is unknown." Inferences that assert that something exists introduce fresh existential variables. — [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)
- **Selected constraints, quoted from the fetched text:**
  - Generation uniqueness (Constraint 24) holds per (entity, activity) pair. An entity may be generated by more than one activity.
  - Generation precedes invalidation (C36). Usage precedes invalidation (C38).
  - Derivation with full arguments: "use1 precedes gen2" (C41). If the activity, usage or generation is unknown: "gen1 strictly precedes gen2" (C42).
  - A derivation that names an activity implies that the activity used the source and generated the derived entity (Inference 11).
  - Specialization implies alternate (Inference 20).
  - Specialization is a strict partial order. Its generation and invalidation must nest inside the general entity's (C45, C46).
  - `alternateOf` is reflexive, symmetric and transitive (Inferences 16–18).
  - Source: [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)

### Inferences
- Mapping to Pneuma: item → Entity; each content version of an item → an Entity that is `specializationOf` the item; scene → Activity; agent → SoftwareAgent that `actedOnBehalfOf` the person; "read" → `used`; "made" → `wasGeneratedBy`; scene card → Bundle (an entity, so the card's own author can be recorded).
- CONTEXT.md principle 5 ("a scene read A and B and made C, so treat C as coming from A and B") is exactly the inference PROV declines to make. PROV-friendly wording: store `used` and `wasGeneratedBy` as facts, and present "C may come from A, B" as a derived view. A recorded `wasDerivedFrom` stays an optional, stronger statement.
- A thread (线索) can be computed as PROV Communication: scene 2 used something that scene 1 generated. PROV-DM defines Communication in those terms, so a thread needs no new primitive.
- Reading an item that later changes means reading a specific version. If records point at the version entity (a content hash), "possibly stale" becomes a comparison between the version that was read and the item's current version.

- I did not fetch PROV-O, the OWL encoding. The relation names above are the same in PROV-O, but I did not check its class and property IRIs.
- I did not check whether W3C has published any newer PROV work after 2013.
- I did not locate in the fetched text the exact inference that derives `wasInformedBy` from generation plus usage. I believe it exists in PROV-CONSTRAINTS, but this is unverified.

## 2. Packaging files plus provenance in a folder: RO-Crate, Workflow Run RO-Crate, CWLProv

### Takeaway
RO-Crate is the closest existing "self-describing folder". It is one JSON-LD file (`ro-crate-metadata.json`) at the root of a directory, the minimum is small, and it explicitly does not need to list every file. Process Run Crate models one tool run as a schema.org `CreateAction` with `object` (inputs) and `result` (outputs). CWLProv takes a different route: a BagIt bag with content-addressed data and a PROV trace.

### Cited Findings
- An RO-Crate's metadata file must contain a descriptor entity of `@type` CreativeWork that "MUST have an about property referencing the Root Data Entity's @id". — [RO-Crate 1.2, Root Data Entity](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html)
- **Root Data Entity minimum:**
  - `@type` MUST be or include `Dataset`.
  - `datePublished` MUST be a single ISO 8601 string.
  - `@id` SHOULD be `./`, the directory that contains the metadata file.
  - `name`, `description` and `license` are SHOULD.
  - Source: [RO-Crate 1.2](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html)
- The metadata document "does not necessarily list or describe all files in the package". Data Entities have no minimum metadata. — [RO-Crate 1.2](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html)
- The RO-Crate site lists 1.3 as the newest release. Process Run Crate 0.6 extends RO-Crate 1.3. I found no release date. — [RO-Crate 1.2 page](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html); [Process Run Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/workflow-run-crate/profiles/process_run_crate/)
- **Process Run Crate 0.6** (`https://w3id.org/ro/wfrun/process/0.6`) describes an "implicit" workflow: tools run by hand or by a script that is not in the crate. — [Process Run Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/workflow-run-crate/profiles/process_run_crate/)
  - The root declares the profile with `conformsTo`.
  - The execution is a `CreateAction`. `UpdateAction` or `ActivateAction` are allowed in specific cases.
  - `instrument` (the tool) is MUST. `object` (inputs) is MAY. `result` (outputs, created or modified) is SHOULD.
  - `agent` and `endTime` are SHOULD. `startTime` is MAY.
  - If `actionStatus` is absent, "consumers should assume that the process completed successfully".
  - Action `@id`s should be unique (random UUIDs are recommended) and listed under the root's `mentions`.
  - Several actions can form an implicit workflow when one action's `result` is another action's `object`.
- **CWLProv** packages a workflow run as a BagIt bag, so any BagIt tool can verify its content and completeness. — [CWLProv repo, bagit.md](https://github.com/common-workflow-language/cwlprov/blob/2131faee01c25acabfb2c0aaf0ad2bbf0e1356f9/bagit.md); [Khan et al. 2019, GigaScience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824458) (search snippet)
  - Provenance goes under `metadata/provenance`, and `primary.cwlprov.provn` is mandatory.
  - The data, including intermediate values, is SHA1-hashed and stored under content-addressed names.
  - The provenance is serialized as PROV-N, PROV-XML, PROV-O (Turtle and N-Triples) and PROV-JSON(-LD).
  - The paper defines four hierarchical levels of provenance. Level 1 is retrospective provenance with content-addressed data.
  - The latest profile release I saw is 0.6.0.
  - Source: [CWLProv repo](https://github.com/common-workflow-language/cwlprov); [BOSC 2018 slides](https://zenodo.org/records/1304969/files/2018-06-25-bosc2018-cwlprov.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- RO-Crate already accepts a crate that does not list every file. That fits Pneuma's "provenance unknown is normal" principle: an unlisted file is not an error.
- Process Run Crate's "implicit workflow" is the same shape as a Pneuma scene: no workflow definition, only a record of what ran, what it read and what it made. It is a plausible export format for scene cards.
- CWLProv shows the alternative: content-addressed copies of every input and intermediate. That gives exact replay at the cost of storage. Pneuma's "keep a reference, optionally copy the whole conversation" choice falls between the two.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Workflow Run Crate or Provenance Run Crate profile pages, so their per-step additions are unverified here.
- I could not retrieve the exact definitions of CWLProv levels 2 to 3.

## 3. Data and ML lineage tools: how inputs and outputs are recorded

### Takeaway
Almost every tool in this group relies on **declaration**: the user or an integration names the inputs. Reads that are not declared are invisible, and none of these tools detects them. DataLad is the instructive exception on the write side: it observes all writes (through version control state) but still relies on declared reads. OpenLineage observes inputs automatically only where an engine exposes its query plan (Spark).

### Cited Findings
- **DataLad `run`.** It records a JSON run record in the commit message (or in a sidecar file under `.datalad/runinfo`). — [DataLad handbook, run](https://handbook.datalad.org/en/latest/basics/101-108-run.html); [datalad-run manual](https://docs.datalad.org/en/stable/generated/man/datalad-run.html)
  - Record fields: `cmd`, `exit`, `pwd`, `dsid`, `inputs`, `outputs`, `extra_inputs`, `chain`.
  - It then runs `datalad save` on "all changes this command triggered in the dataset – be that new files or changes to existing files".
  - If the command changes nothing, no record is written.
- In DataLad, `--input` and `--output` mainly drive preparation: declared inputs are retrieved (`get`) before the run, and declared outputs are unlocked or removed. `--explicit` treats the declarations as complete: it suppresses the dirty-repository warning and saves only the listed outputs. By default, every change is saved. If the command fails, nothing is saved unless `--on-cmd-failure` says otherwise. — [datalad-run manual](https://docs.datalad.org/en/stable/generated/man/datalad-run.html)
- In the handbook's example, `outputs` is empty even though the command created `recordings/podcasts.tsv`. The file was still saved and linked to the command by the commit. — [DataLad handbook, run](https://handbook.datalad.org/en/latest/basics/101-108-run.html)
- **`datalad rerun`** re-executes the run record of a given commit. "If a `datalad rerun` does not add or change any content in the dataset, it will also not be recorded in the history." It compares the new exit code with the recorded one. The handbook suggests `datalad diff`, `git diff` and `git log -- <file>` to see what changed. It does not describe any check of "outputs older than inputs". — [DataLad handbook, rerun](https://handbook.datalad.org/en/latest/basics/101-109-rerun.html); [datalad-run manual](https://docs.datalad.org/en/stable/generated/man/datalad-run.html)
- **DVC.** A stage in `dvc.yaml` must have a `cmd`. `deps`, `outs` and `params` are declared by the user. `dvc.lock` records a content hash (`md5`, `etag` or `checksum`) for each dependency and output, plus the parameter values. With it, DVC "detect[s] when stage definitions, or their dependencies have changed". — [DVC dvc.yaml docs](https://doc.dvc.org/user-guide/project-structure/dvcyaml-files)
  - DVC does not document what happens when a command reads a file that is not in `deps`. It advises code to "Read/write exclusively from/to the specified dependencies and outputs".
  - `always_changed: true` makes a stage always count as changed (a volatile task).
  - `frozen` prevents execution during reproduction.
  - `persist: false` (the default) deletes outputs before `repro`.
- **Pachyderm** (now part of HPE): pipelines "automatically record provenance as they execute", because a pipeline's inputs are declared input repos and its outputs go to an output repo named after the pipeline. Commit provenance links commits across repos. A **global ID** is shared by every commit and job downstream of one commit. — [Pachyderm 2.8 provenance](https://docs.pachyderm.com/2.8.x/learn/glossary/provenance); [Pachyderm 2.6 global ID](https://docs.pachyderm.com/2.6.x/learn/glossary/globalid) (search snippet); [HPE MLDM glossary](https://docs.ai-solutions.ext.hpe.com/products/mldm/latest/learn/glossary)
- **lakeFS** provides immutable commits on object storage. Its lineage pattern is a convention: store the job's code version (for example a Git hash) as commit metadata and pin reads to a commit. These are vendor-described claims. — [lakeFS reproducibility docs](https://docs.lakefs.io/use-cases/reproducibility/); [lakeFS blog, lineage](https://lakefs.io/blog/data-lineage-for-data-lakes/) (search snippet)
- **MLflow.** `mlflow.log_input(dataset, context=...)` declares a dataset as a run input. It can log metadata only, without the data. `mlflow.evaluate()` logs its dataset automatically. — [MLflow dataset tracking](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/dataset/); [Databricks KB](https://kb.databricks.com/machine-learning/lineage-for-the-output-table-with-predictions-is-not-tracked-in-mlflow-when-training-a-model); [Fabric MLflow 3 overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/fabric/data-science/mlflow-3-overview) (search snippet)
  - A Databricks KB article says there is "no built-in method in MLflow to log output tables, similar to mlflow.log_input for input tables".
  - In MLflow 3, `log_model()` creates a LoggedModel linked to its source run and its training datasets.
- **Weights & Biases.** Lineage edges come only from `run.use_artifact()` (input) and `run.log_artifact()` (output). The graph is a DAG of runs and artifacts, and the SDK can traverse it with `logged_by()` and `used_by()`. A third-party tutorial says a run that reads a local file directly shows no dependency in the graph. — [W&B docs, artifact graph](https://docs.wandb.ai/models/artifacts/explore-and-traverse-an-artifact-graph) (search snippet); [third-party tutorial](https://theneuralbase.com/weights-biases/learn/intermediate/artifact-dependencies-and-lineage/) (secondary, unverified against official docs)
- **OpenLineage** (spec 1.55.0 per the docs selector). Core objects are Job, Run (a client-generated UUID, UUIDv7 recommended) and Dataset (a name unique within a namespace derived from its physical location). — [OpenLineage object model](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model)
  - RunEvents carry a state, usually START then COMPLETE. Updates are additive: inputs can be sent with START, with COMPLETE, or with both.
  - Facets extend each object. Dataset facets hold cross-run metadata such as schema. Input and output facets hold per-run details.
  - A `parent` run facet links a run to the run that spawned it.
  - **JobEvent** carries static, design-time metadata including declared inputs and outputs, "not associated with a Run". **DatasetEvent** attaches metadata outside any run.
- The OpenLineage Spark integration registers as a SparkListener and walks Spark's logical plan to extract inputs and outputs, so this capture is observed and not declared. Coverage depends on the plan node types the integration supports. — [OpenLineage Spark built-in lineage](https://openlineage.io/docs/development/developing/spark/built_in_lineage) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Two capture styles appear. (a) Declared: DVC, MLflow, W&B, DataLad `--input`, OpenLineage JobEvent. (b) Observed at a layer that already sees everything: DataLad's "save all changes" (the VCS sees writes), Pachyderm (the platform mediates all I/O), OpenLineage Spark (the engine's plan). Pneuma's analogue of (b) is the library seeing writes into its own folder and, if the agent goes through the CLI, seeing reads. Reads made directly from disk are the blind spot, as they are for every tool here.
- DataLad's handling of undeclared outputs (saved anyway and attached to the command by the commit) is a useful precedent. Pneuma can attach every new or changed item in a scene to that scene even when the agent declared nothing.
- OpenLineage's split between design-time declarations (JobEvent) and run-time observations (RunEvent), and its additive partial events, fit "scene cards are append-only, one file per scene".
- `datalad rerun` writes nothing when content is unchanged. That is early cutoff at commit granularity (see section 4).

### Gaps
- I did not verify W&B behaviour for undeclared reads from official docs. Only a secondary tutorial covers it.
- I did not check lakeFS for any first-class lineage feature beyond commit metadata. Its glossary lists "Data Lineage" as a feature, but I did not see how it works.
- I did not confirm how a LoggedModel's dataset links relate to run-level `log_input` records in MLflow.
- I did not look up OpenLineage integrations other than Spark (Airflow, dbt) to see whether they observe or declare.

## 4. Build systems: defining and detecting "out of date"

### Takeaway
"Build Systems à la Carte" gives the cleanest formal statement. A non-input key is up to date if recomputing its task against the current store yields exactly the stored value. Real systems approximate this in one of four ways: a dirty bit (Make's timestamps, Excel), verifying traces (hashes of the dependencies used last time; Shake, Ninja), constructive traces (also store results; Bazel, CloudBuild), or deep constructive traces (hash only terminal inputs; Nix, Buck). Pneuma cannot recompute, because agents are not deterministic functions, so only the verifying-trace reading fits: "the inputs this item was made from no longer have the hashes recorded when it was made".

### Cited Findings
- Venue: Mokhov, Mitchell, Peyton Jones, "Build Systems à la Carte", Proc. ACM Program. Lang. 2 (ICFP), Article 79, September 2018. The extended version is "Build systems à la carte: Theory and practice", JFP 30, e11, 2020, open access. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf); [JFP 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-functional-programming/article/build-systems-a-la-carte-theory-and-practice/097CE52C750E69BD16B78C318754C7A4)
- **Minimality (Def. 2.1):** "A build system is minimal if it executes tasks at most once per build and only if they transitively depend on inputs that changed since the previous build." — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Correctness (Def. 3.1):** for `result = build tasks key store`, split the keys reachable from the target into inputs I and non-inputs O. The result is correct if (1) for all k ∈ I, `getValue k result == getValue k store` ("no inputs were corrupted"), and (2) for all k ∈ O, `getValue k result == compute task result`. The intuition is given in words: "up to date" means "if we recompute the value of the key (using the task description, and the final store), we should get exactly the same value as we see in the final store". — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- For non-deterministic tasks, the condition relaxes to `getValue k result ∈ computeND task result`: the stored value must be one possible result. **Volatile** tasks (RANDBETWEEN, phony rules) are "best modelled as depending on a special key RealWorld whose value is changed in every build". "Tasks that do have untracked dependencies can be marked as volatile … removing the untracked dependency at the cost of minimality." Bazel's sandboxing guards against missing dependencies, but "none are likely to capture all dependencies right down to the CPU model and microcode version". — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Dirty bit.** There is one persistent bit per key, and a key needs no rebuild if it and all its transitive dependencies are clean. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
  - Excel marks dirty every cell that transitively depends on a changed cell, using a static over-approximation: it follows both branches of IF and treats INDIRECT as "depends on anything".
  - Make's modification times act as "a dirty bit which is set when a file is older than its dependencies". This bit is not under Make's control. Make "requires that file timestamps only go forward in time, which can be violated by backup software", and `touch` fools it, so "Make is therefore minimal only under the assumption that you do not do that".
- **Verifying traces.** For each key, record the hash of its result and the (dependency, hash) pairs it read. Later, the key is dirty if any recorded dependency hash differs from the current one. Signatures: `recordVT :: k -> Hash v -> [(k, Hash v)] -> VT k v -> VT k v` and `verifyVT :: … -> m Bool`. Traces "support dynamic dependencies and minimality", and all trace types except deep traces support early cutoff. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Constructive traces** also store the resulting value, so the result can be fetched from a shared cache without rebuilding (cloud builds). **Deep constructive traces** record only hashes of terminal inputs. When they skip two or more dependency levels, the tasks must be deterministic, and early cutoff is lost. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Early cutoff.** "When it executes a task and the result is unchanged from the previous build, it is unnecessary to execute the dependent tasks." Make and Excel do not support it. Shake and Bazel do. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Static vs dynamic dependencies.** `Task Applicative` can have only static dependencies, which can be extracted without running the task. `Task Monad` allows dynamic dependencies, which are discovered while the task runs, as in Excel's INDIRECT or Shake's `need` after reading a file. — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Classification (Table 2), rebuilder × scheduler.** — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
  - Dirty bit: Make (topological), Excel (restarting).
  - Verifying traces: Ninja (topological), Shake (suspending).
  - Constructive traces: CloudBuild (topological), Bazel (restarting).
  - Deep constructive traces: Buck (topological), Nix (suspending).
  - Text notes:
    - Redo "almost exactly matches Shake".
    - Pluto is like Shake but allows cyclic rules with a user resolution strategy.
    - Tup is like Make "with a refined dirty-bit implementation that watches the file system for changes", and it "automatically deletes stale results".
- **Fabricate** is the one system the authors could not model. A Fabricate build is a script run in order, with no declaration of what each line produces. Each command "is traced at the OS-level", for example "gcc -c util.c reads from util.c", and later runs skip commands whose inputs have not changed. The pure model is imperfect because "gcc writes to arbitrary files whose locations are not known in advance". — [ICFP 2018 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Tup** uses FUSE on Linux and macOS to observe the reads and writes of each command. It reports an error when a command reads a file that the rule does not declare as an input. `--ghosts` shows "files that a command tried to access, but don't actually exist". Users report false input dependencies under Fuse-T and occasional unmount failures. — [gittup blog, missing dependencies](https://gittup.org/blog/2014/05/7-clobber-builds-part-2---fixing-missing-dependencies); [tup(1)](https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/jammy/man1/tup.1.html); [jenga issue #23](https://github.com/janestreet/jenga/issues/23) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Cleanest statement for Pneuma, adapted from verifying traces: a made item version V is **possibly stale** iff some entity E recorded as read in V's scene now has a current content hash ≠ the hash recorded at read time. That is a deterministic check and needs no judgment, which matches CONTEXT.md principle 4. "Possibly" is the right word: Pneuma cannot run Def. 3.1's `compute`, and the agent is a non-deterministic task in the paper's sense.
- **Transitive propagation is a design choice the paper makes visible.** A dirty bit (Excel) marks all transitive dependents. Verifying traces check only direct dependencies, and early cutoff stops propagation when a remade intermediate turns out unchanged. For Pneuma, which never remakes anything, the question is whether to show "possibly stale because an upstream item is possibly stale". The paper's framing suggests showing direct staleness as fact and transitive staleness as a separate, derived flag.
- **Use content hashes, not timestamps.** Make's weaknesses (backup software, `touch`, clocks only going forward) are exactly what a Dropbox-style sync plugin causes.
- An agent session is a Fabricate-style build: an ordered script, OS-level reads, outputs not declared in advance. That is the one model that does not fit the paper's framework cleanly.
- Tup's "ghost" nodes (attempted reads of files that did not exist) are a negative dependency. If a scene looked for X and did not find it, adding X later could make the scene's output possibly stale.

### Gaps
- I read only the ICFP 2018 version. I did not check whether the 2020 JFP version changes the definitions or adds systems to the table.
- I did not read Shake's, Pluto's or redo's own papers or docs. Their positions come only from this paper.

## 5. Supply-chain attestation (in-toto, SLSA, Sigstore) and C2PA content credentials

### Takeaway
Both families use the same shape: **subject** (identified by digest) + **predicate type** + **predicate**. For SLSA the predicate holds the build definition (external parameters and resolved dependencies) and the run details (builder, invocation, byproducts). A signature wraps the statement. C2PA adds two ideas Pneuma can use: typed ingredient relationships (`parentOf` / `componentOf` / `inputTo`), and a split between assertions the signer vouches for and assertions it only relays.

### Cited Findings
- **in-toto Statement v1:**
  - `_type` = `https://in-toto.io/Statement/v1`.
  - `subject`: an array of ResourceDescriptors, each with a required `digest` and an optional `name`.
  - `predicateType`: a type URI.
  - `predicate`: an optional object.
  - "Subject artifacts are matched purely by digest, regardless of content type." Subjects are assumed to be immutable. `name` is optional, and producers may set it to `_` when it has no meaning.
  - Source: [in-toto Statement v1](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/v1/statement.md)
- **SLSA Provenance.** The predicate URI `https://slsa.dev/provenance/v1` is version-independent. The v1.1 page says "Version 1.2 is the current version" and marks v1.1 as retired. — [SLSA v1.1 provenance](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)
  - `buildDefinition`:
    - `buildType`: the template that says how to read the rest.
    - `externalParameters`: these "MUST be included in the provenance and MUST be verified downstream".
    - `internalParameters`: set by the platform; optional.
    - `resolvedDependencies`: artifacts fetched during the build.
  - `runDetails`:
    - `builder.id`: "the sole determiner of the SLSA Build level".
    - `builder.version` and `builderDependencies`.
    - `metadata`: `invocationId`, `startedOn`, `finishedOn`.
    - `byproducts`: for example logs; not outputs.
- **SLSA completeness.** For `resolvedDependencies`, "Completeness is best effort, at least through SLSA Build L3." Trust rests on the builder: it is "trusted to faithfully run the build and record the provenance", and "Consumers MUST accept only specific signer-builder pairs." — [SLSA v1.1 provenance](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)
- **Sigstore**, from secondary sources only. A DSSE envelope wraps the in-toto statement and is signed with a short-lived Fulcio certificate. The signature is recorded in the Rekor transparency log, and a Sigstore bundle packages the certificate, the signature and the log receipts. Verifiers check the Fulcio chain, the Rekor inclusion proof, the DSSE signature, and that the subject digest matches the artifact. — [Socket glossary](https://socket.dev/glossary/sigstore); [sigstore Go library docs](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/git-pkgs/sigstore) (secondary; I did not check the Sigstore bundle spec)
- **C2PA 2.2** (May 2025; the site lists versions up to 2.5): assertions + claim + claim signature form a C2PA Manifest. — [C2PA 2.2 spec](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)
  - The claim (`c2pa.claim.v2`) lists `created_assertions` (attributed to the signer), `gathered_assertions` (from other workflow components such as human-entered information, *not* attributed to the signer), and `redacted_assertions`.
  - A standard manifest needs exactly one hard binding (for example `c2pa.hash.data`) and an actions assertion.
  - Redaction removes or zeroes an assertion, adds a record to `redacted_assertions`, and SHOULD add a `c2pa.redacted` action. Actions and hard bindings cannot be redacted.
  - A validator may report `ingredient.unknownProvenance` or `manifest.unknownProvenance`. I saw these codes only in the spec's versioning section, so their exact semantics are unverified.
- **C2PA ingredients** (search snippet):
  - Each ingredient is documented as exactly one of `parentOf` (the asset was derived from it; at most one; it pairs with a first action `c2pa.opened`), `componentOf` (the ingredient is part of the asset; the default; it pairs with `c2pa.placed`), or `inputTo` (input to a computational process that created or modified the asset).
  - An ingredient that carries its own manifest records it as `active_manifest`.
  - Writers should use `c2pa.ingredient.v3`.
  - Every manifest starts with `c2pa.created` or `c2pa.opened`.
  - Actions reference ingredients by their instance IDs.
  - Sources: [CAI docs, writing ingredients](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/manifest/writing/ingredients); [CAI docs, actions](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/manifest/assertions-actions)

### Inferences
- The statement shape maps onto a Pneuma record: subject = the made item version (by content hash); predicate = the scene: agent (builder), invocation or session ID, start and end, things read (resolvedDependencies), byproducts (scratch files, logs).
- SLSA's "best effort completeness" is an explicit precedent for treating the read list as possibly incomplete.
- C2PA's created vs gathered split suits Pneuma. The library can mark what it observed itself (for example a write into the folder) separately from what an agent or person told it (for example "I also read this web page").
- C2PA's three ingredient roles are a vocabulary for strengthening the coarse "made in the same scene" link: "edited from" (`parentOf`), "embedded or placed" (`componentOf`), "used as input" (`inputTo`).
- Signing is out of scope for Pneuma (local convention, not enforcement). The digest-addressed subject is still worth copying, because it makes records survive renames and sync.

### Gaps
- I did not read the C2PA ingredient section in full, because the 2.2 spec page is about 580k characters. How an ingredient without its own credentials is recorded, and what `unknownProvenance` means exactly, remain unverified.
- I did not fetch the SLSA v1.2 page. I assumed the predicate fields are unchanged from v1.1 because the URI is version-independent, but I did not check.

## 6. Automatic capture of reads without the program's cooperation

### Takeaway
OS-level read capture is mature on **Linux** (ptrace: strace, ReproZip, CARE, rr; LD_PRELOAD: fsatrace, PROBE; kernel: CamFlow, audit, eBPF) but costly. In a 2024 benchmark, ptrace collectors added roughly 45 to 193% wall time (geometric mean) and only library interposition (fsatrace) came near zero. On **macOS** a developer laptop is hostile to every approach: SIP strips `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` from protected binaries such as `/bin/sh`, fsatrace needs SIP disabled, Endpoint Security needs a restricted Apple entitlement, and ReproZip packs only on Linux. Application-level capture (noWorkflow for Python) avoids the OS but sees only one runtime.

### Cited Findings
- **ReproZip.** `reprozip trace` runs the command under ptrace, traces all system calls and stores them in SQLite. Packing works only for experiments that ran on Linux, and it cannot trace across machines. — [ReproZip docs](https://reprozip.readthedocs.io/en/1.x/packing.html); [ReproZip FAQ](https://github.com/ViDA-NYU/reprozip/blob/1.0.x/docs/faq.rst) (search snippet)
- **Grayson et al., ACM REP 2024** ("A benchmark suite and performance analysis of user-space provenance collectors", doi 10.1145/3641525.3663627). — [paper PDF](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GraysonETAL24ProvenanceCollectorsSurvey.pdf)
  - Abstract: "System-level provenance collectors can track provenance data without requiring the user to change anything about their application. However, system-level provenance collectors have performance overheads."
  - Reproduced: strace, fsatrace, rr, ReproZip and CARE, all "tracing".
  - Partially reproduced and then excluded: Sciunit, PTU, CDE, ltrace.
  - "Needs more time": eBPF/bpftrace, DTrace, SystemTap, SPADE, PROV-IO.
  - "Requires custom kernel": CamFlow, Hi-Fi, LPM/ProvMon and others.
  - ETW and Sysmon: "Not for Linux".
- **Overhead table (Table 4, geometric mean of wall-time overhead, columns as printed):** — [Grayson et al. 2024](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GraysonETAL24ProvenanceCollectorsSurvey.pdf)
  - Overall: fsatrace 0%, CARE 45%, strace 66%, rr 146%, ReproZip 193%.
  - Notebook data science row: fsatrace 4%, CARE 15%, strace 32%, rr 20%, ReproZip 174%.
  - `cp` row: 37 / 641 / 380 / 232 / 5791% in the same column order.
  - Caveat: I took the column order from the extracted table header and did not check it against the rendered table.
- **Conclusions of Grayson et al. 2024:** — [Grayson et al. 2024](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GraysonETAL24ProvenanceCollectorsSurvey.pdf)
  - "Current provenance collectors are too slow for 'always on'". The exception is fsatrace, which is fast because it "uses library interpositioning rather than ptrace" but is "one of the weakest collectors": it records only file reads, writes, moves, deletes, queries and touches, with nothing on forks and execs.
  - Collectors are either *monitoring* (only log operations: fsatrace, strace, ltrace) or *interrupting* (stop the process to copy files: ReproZip, Sciunit, rr, CARE, CDE).
  - Security tools must resist adversarial programs. "Computational science, on the other hand, may [be] satisfied by a solution that can be intentionally circumvented by an uncooperative program but would work most of the time."
  - Proposed optimisation: "copy-on-write-after-read". Copy a read file lazily, only before it gets mutated.
  - "eBPF requires privileges … library interposition assumes the tracee only uses libc."
- **PROBE** (Provenance for Replay OBservation Engine) records a program's inputs and outputs via `LD_PRELOAD` and can export a dataflow graph. Its README calls the provenance features "a huge work in progress". — [PROBE repo](https://github.com/charmoniumQ/PROBE) (search snippet)
- **Whole-system kernel capture.** CamFlow is a self-contained Linux kernel module. — [CamFlow, SoCC 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05296); [Kernel telemetry study, arXiv 2608.11418](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11418) (search snippet; I did not confirm its peer-review status)
  - A 2026 arXiv study reports CamFlow overhead of 45% on file workloads and 55% on network workloads, with 95–96% log loss in two workloads.
  - The same study reports SPADE on auditd at 13.4% process overhead with 91–99% loss.
  - It reports that eAudit (eBPF) is efficient, at about 4.5% overhead, but does not build a provenance graph.
- **noWorkflow** captures provenance of Python scripts "without requiring users to change the way they work". It uses AST analysis, reflection and profiling, plus definition provenance from the AST and bytecode. Its authors note that system-call approaches "do not have visibility into what happens inside the scripts". — [Murta et al., IPAW 2014](https://braganholo.github.io/papers/murta2014-ipaw.pdf) (search snippet)
- **fsatrace** "injects code into other applications in order to trace file accesses" (r/w/m/d/q/t records). It supports Unix, macOS and Windows, but on macOS newer than 10.10 "System Integrity Protection must be disabled". It is intended to help build systems generate or verify dependencies. — [fsatrace README](https://github.com/jacereda/fsatrace)
- **macOS SIP and dyld.** The dynamic linker ignores `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` for SIP-protected executables such as `/bin/sh` and `/usr/bin/env`. Shell scripts therefore lose the injected library, and the workarounds copy `/bin/sh` elsewhere. — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731358); [mirrord write-up](https://dev.to/aviramha/fun-with-macoss-sip-2646) (search snippet)
- **macOS Endpoint Security.** The client needs the restricted `com.apple.developer.endpoint-security.client` entitlement, authorized through a provisioning profile. `es_event_open_t` identifies the opened file, and `AUTH_OPEN` lets a client allow or deny the open. On macOS 26 a file copy produces extra `AUTH_OPEN` events on directories with `O_SEARCH` in `fflag`, and Apple said the change was deliberate. — [Apple forums, entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791996); [es_event_open_t](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/endpointsecurity/es_event_open_t.md); [Apple forums, macOS 26 change](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/796808) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- On a Mac laptop, transparent OS-level read capture for arbitrary agent processes is not practical without either an Apple-approved Endpoint Security entitlement (a signed, notarized system extension) or disabling SIP. FUSE-based capture (as in Tup) needs a FUSE implementation and has had false dependencies and unmount problems on macOS.
- The practical source of read events for Pneuma is the agent layer. The agent's own tool calls (file reads, writes, shell commands) are an application-level trace, analogous to noWorkflow. Such a trace is circumventable (a shell command's internal reads are not seen), but Grayson et al. argue that "works most of the time, can be circumvented" is acceptable outside security. This matches CONTEXT.md item 10 (convention, not enforcement). I did not research whether specific agent products expose such hooks.
- "Copy-on-write-after-read" from Grayson et al. maps to Pneuma's optional copy: to keep exactly what a scene read, snapshot it lazily and only when the source is about to change.

### Gaps
- I found no benchmark of read capture on macOS (Endpoint Security, fs_usage, DTrace under SIP). All numbers above are for Linux.
- I did not verify Sciunit's and CDE's current maintenance status beyond "partially reproduced" in Grayson et al. 2024.
- The eBPF numbers come from one 2026 arXiv study whose peer-review status I could not confirm.

## 7. How the models represent "provenance unknown" or incomplete provenance

### Takeaway
No standard has a first-class "unknown" node. Each one instead (a) makes most arguments optional, (b) introduces anonymous existential placeholders, (c) declares completeness best-effort, or (d) lets a package omit things without that being an error. Only C2PA validators appear to emit an explicit "unknown provenance" status, and I could only partly verify that.

### Cited Findings
- PROV: the activity, generation and usage in a derivation are optional. Attribution exists for when the activity is unknown. PROV-N writes `-` for an absent argument. Normalization fills unknowns with existential variables ("exists, but its exact identity is unknown"). Instances "need not be valid". — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)
- PROV keeps a weaker ordering constraint when derivation details are unknown: C42 requires only that the source's generation strictly precedes the derived entity's generation. — [PROV-CONSTRAINTS](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)
- RO-Crate: the metadata "does not necessarily list or describe all files", and Data Entities have no minimum metadata. Process Run Crate: `object` (inputs) is only MAY, and a missing `actionStatus` means success. — [RO-Crate 1.2](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html); [Process Run Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/workflow-run-crate/profiles/process_run_crate/)
- SLSA: `resolvedDependencies` completeness is "best effort, at least through SLSA Build L3". — [SLSA v1.1](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)
- C2PA: assertions can be redacted, and the redaction itself is recorded. Gathered assertions are carried but not vouched for. Validators may emit `ingredient.unknownProvenance` or `manifest.unknownProvenance` (exact semantics unverified). — [C2PA 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)
- DataLad: files saved by plain `datalad save`, and not by `run`, have no run record. In a `run` without `--explicit`, undeclared outputs are still saved under the run's commit. — [datalad-run manual](https://docs.datalad.org/en/stable/generated/man/datalad-run.html)
- Build systems: untracked dependencies are handled by marking the task volatile (it depends on `RealWorld`), at the cost of minimality. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- W&B and MLflow: an undeclared input simply has no edge. MLflow has no built-in way to log output tables. — [W&B tutorial (secondary)](https://theneuralbase.com/weights-biases/learn/intermediate/artifact-dependencies-and-lineage/); [Databricks KB](https://kb.databricks.com/machine-learning/lineage-for-the-output-table-with-predictions-is-not-tracked-in-mlflow-when-training-a-model)

### Inferences
- Pneuma's "来历不明" (unknown provenance) needs at least three distinct states that these systems blur:
  1. **Unrecorded**: a file appeared or changed with no scene recorded. Analogous to a DataLad commit without a run record.
  2. **Recorded but possibly incomplete**: a scene exists, but its read list is best-effort. Analogous to SLSA `resolvedDependencies`.
  3. **Asserted, not observed**: an agent or person stated the provenance. Analogous to a C2PA gathered assertion.
- The PROV existential-variable idea gives a clean way to say "this file was made by *some* activity we do not know" without inventing a fake scene.
- The build-systems answer to untracked dependencies (treat as volatile, always possibly stale) is the conservative default for items whose read set is unknown.

### Gaps
- I found no standard with an explicit, first-class "provenance unknown" value apart from the partly verified C2PA validator status codes.

## 8. Notebook and document lineage (distinct ideas only)

### Takeaway
One distinct idea: IPyflow (formerly nbsafety) detects **stale cells** with per-symbol timestamps and *highlights* them, without re-running unless the user opts into reactive mode. That is "mark possibly stale, don't redo" in an interactive tool.

### Cited Findings
- IPyflow tracks dataflow between symbols and cells within a session. A symbol is stale when one of its dependencies has a newer timestamp than the symbol itself. Staleness detection, reactive scheduling, slicing and memoization all read from one dataflow graph. — [IPyflow dataflow model](https://ipyflow.readthedocs.io/en/latest/concepts/dataflow_model.html); [IPyflow repo](https://github.com/ipyflow/ipyflow) (search snippet)
- Cells with live references to stale symbols get red highlights. Reactivity is opt-in. In reactive mode, the minimal set of out-of-sync cells re-executes. — [IPyflow on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ipyflow/0.0.137) (search snippet)
- The nbsafety paper replayed 666 real notebook sessions and found 117 with potential safety errors. — [nbsafety, arXiv 2012.06981](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06981.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- IPyflow uses timestamps safely only because it controls the clock inside one kernel. Across a synced folder, the build-systems findings argue for hashes.
- I did not research backlink tools (Roam, Logseq) or Observable. Backlinks are a computed reverse index, which Pneuma already has in the "library as reverse index" idea. I found nothing to suggest they add a lineage idea beyond that.

### Gaps
- I did not research Observable's reactive runtime or Jupyter provenance extensions other than IPyflow.

## Ideas worth borrowing and traps to avoid

### Borrow
- **One entity per version, plus a "specialization" link to the enduring item.** Records then point at what was actually read. — PROV `specializationOf` / `alternateOf` ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/))
- **Store `used` and `generated` as facts, and treat "derived from" as a separate, optional, stronger claim.** PROV says a usage-then-generation chain is necessary but not sufficient for derivation. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Scene card as a bundle.** It is an entity in its own right, so the card can carry who wrote it. — PROV Bundle ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/))
- **Thread as Communication.** It is derived from "B used what A generated" and needs no new primitive. — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **"Possibly stale" as a failed verifying trace.** Record (input, content hash) at read time. The item is possibly stale iff a current hash differs. This is deterministic and supports dynamic dependencies. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Volatile marking for unknown read sets.** Treat an item with untracked inputs as depending on `RealWorld`. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Save every write in a scene, even undeclared ones, and attach it to the scene.** — DataLad `run` default behaviour ([datalad-run manual](https://docs.datalad.org/en/stable/generated/man/datalad-run.html))
- **Write no record when nothing changed.** — `datalad run` / `rerun` ([DataLad handbook](https://handbook.datalad.org/en/latest/basics/101-109-rerun.html))
- **Additive, partial events with a stable run ID**, and design-time declarations kept apart from run-time observations. — OpenLineage RunEvent vs JobEvent ([OpenLineage](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model))
- **Folder-level self-description in one JSON-LD file**, a tiny minimum, and no requirement to list every file. **A run as `CreateAction{instrument, object, result, agent, endTime}`**, which gives an export target. — [RO-Crate 1.2](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification/1.2/root-data-entity.html); [Process Run Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/workflow-run-crate/profiles/process_run_crate/)
- **Subjects identified by digest**, so records survive renames and sync. — [in-toto Statement](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/v1/statement.md)
- **Declare completeness honestly ("best effort").** — [SLSA](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)
- **Keep what the library observed apart from what someone told it.** — C2PA created vs gathered assertions ([C2PA 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html))
- **Typed roles for a strengthened link:** parentOf / componentOf / inputTo. — [CAI docs](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/manifest/writing/ingredients)
- **Ghost dependencies:** files a scene looked for but did not find. — Tup `--ghosts` ([tup(1)](https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/jammy/man1/tup.1.html))
- **Copy-on-write-after-read** for the optional "copy what was read". — [Grayson et al. 2024](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GraysonETAL24ProvenanceCollectorsSurvey.pdf)
- **Mark stale and highlight, don't redo.** — IPyflow non-reactive mode ([IPyflow](https://ipyflow.readthedocs.io/en/latest/concepts/dataflow_model.html))

### Avoid
- **Timestamps as the staleness signal.** Make breaks under backup software, `touch` and clocks that do not only move forward, all of which a sync plugin will cause. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Inferring derivation from co-occurrence in a scene and storing it as fact.** — [PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)
- **Assuming declared inputs are complete.** DVC, MLflow and W&B have no edge for an undeclared read, and none of them detects one. — [DVC](https://doc.dvc.org/user-guide/project-structure/dvcyaml-files); [W&B tutorial (secondary)](https://theneuralbase.com/weights-biases/learn/intermediate/artifact-dependencies-and-lineage/)
- **Depending on OS-level read tracing on macOS.** SIP strips dyld injection, fsatrace needs SIP off, Endpoint Security needs a restricted entitlement, and ReproZip packs only on Linux. — [fsatrace](https://github.com/jacereda/fsatrace); [Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731358); [Apple forums, ES entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791996); [ReproZip](https://reprozip.readthedocs.io/en/1.x/packing.html)
- **Expecting "always on" system-level capture to be cheap.** ptrace collectors add about 45–193% wall time (geometric mean, Linux). — [Grayson et al. 2024](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GraysonETAL24ProvenanceCollectorsSurvey.pdf)
- **Deep, input-only hashing (Nix and Buck style) for staleness.** It needs deterministic tasks and gives up early cutoff. Agents are not deterministic. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Transitive dirty marking by static over-approximation (Excel style) without saying so.** It marks more than necessary. If Pneuma propagates "possibly stale" transitively, it should label that flag as derived. — [Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf)
- **Overstating what a record proves.** SLSA provenance is only as good as the builder that wrote it. A Pneuma record written by an agent is an assertion, not an observation. — [SLSA](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)
